# UMADA — Episode 1 Interactive Choice Architecture Brief

**Artifact under review:** `episode-cape-wipeout-pt1-interactive.html`  
**Purpose:** Revise the Episode 1 reader so audience choice is a core narrative system rather than a decorative feature.  
**Primary principle:** The audience may choose how to investigate history, but they may not rewrite what historically occurred.

---

## 1. Core Direction

Episode 1 should not become a fully linear story with occasional buttons.

Interactivity is part of the Omoluabi method.

The audience should be able to choose:

- which evidence to examine;
- which witness to follow;
- which location to enter;
- which contradiction to investigate;
- which provisional conclusion to hold;
- which unresolved questions to preserve.

The audience should not be able to alter locked canon.

The system must distinguish between:

> **Interpretive agency**

and:

> **Authorial control over history**

The audience is a witness-investigator, not the author of the Cape Wipeout.

---

## 2. What Audience Choice Should Control

### 2.1 Evidence channel

At major moments, allow the audience to choose between evidence types.

Recommended evidence channels:

- **BODY** — physical condition, sensory evidence, survival signs.
- **RECORD** — official status, timestamps, military files, institutional claims.
- **WITNESS** — testimony or perception from Ada, Tallai, Q, Quartz, or the Reporter.
- **ENVIRONMENT** — garden sensors, weather systems, damaged infrastructure, biological evidence.
- **DEVICE** — anomalies registered by the Omoluabi device.

These choices should reveal different fragments and contradictions.

They should not all produce the same paragraph under different labels.

---

### 2.2 Route through Friedmandorstrop

After the Council prologue and device transfer, allow the audience to choose where to direct the witness record.

Example prompt:

> **WHERE DO YOU DIRECT THE OMOLUABI RECORD?**

Choices:

- Follow Tallai to the bread stop.
- Enter the laboratory garden.
- Locate Ada.
- Inspect the Reporter’s device anomaly.

Each route should establish different knowledge before the five ticks converge.

#### Tallai route

May reveal:

- the familiar bread route;
- Tallai’s relationship to the town;
- Q and Quartz’s ordinary behavior;
- civilian trust;
- Tallai’s care practices before they become doctrine.

#### Garden route

May reveal:

- experimental ecosystems;
- scientists and families;
- sensor disturbances;
- the relationship between ecology and technology;
- Q detecting an anomaly before official systems do.

#### Ada route

May reveal:

- Ada arriving early;
- Ada waiting for KC;
- military procedure;
- Ada’s emotional and bodily state;
- the premature KIA record.

#### Reporter route

May reveal:

- device instability;
- temporal distortion;
- incomplete visual access;
- the Reporter’s role as observer;
- evidence the Reporter cannot interpret yet.

All paths eventually converge at Tick Four, Tick Five, and BOOM.

---

### 2.3 Witness perspective after the blast

After BOOM, allow the audience to choose whose signal to follow.

Example prompt:

> **WHICH SIGNAL DO YOU FOLLOW?**

Choices:

- Q’s detection signal.
- Quartz’s rescue path.
- Tallai’s care decision.
- Ada’s bodily memory.
- The official incident record.
- The Reporter’s damaged witness feed.

Each perspective should reveal a different part of the rescue.

#### Q

Focus:

- conflicting signals;
- sensory detection;
- life signs;
- contradiction between body and record;
- urgency before explanation.

#### Quartz

Focus:

- debris;
- weight;
- physical rescue;
- gentleness;
- the dusty teddy bear;
- carrying both survivor and civilian memory.

#### Tallai

Focus:

- decision-making;
- rescue authority;
- care ethics;
- refusal to accept the record as final;
- early Rescue Doctrine.

#### Ada

Focus:

- fragmented bodily awareness;
- pain;
- the KIA designation;
- disorientation;
- her question about KC.

#### Official record

Focus:

- institutional language;
- unchanged KIA status;
- timestamps;
- missing amendments;
- evidence suppression or procedural failure.

#### Reporter

Focus:

- damaged device;
- partial observation;
- inability to intervene;
- the ethics of witnessing;
- temporal instability.

## 3. Choice Under Contradiction

The BODY / RECORD mechanic should expand into a larger contradiction system.

Example:

> **OFFICIAL RECORD:** KIA  
> **BODY SIGNAL:** RESPONSIVE  
> **WITNESS SIGNAL:** EXTRACTION ORDERED  
> **ENVIRONMENTAL SIGNAL:** BLAST ZONE UNSTABLE

Prompt:

> **WHICH CHANNEL SHOULD THE DEVICE PRIORITIZE?**

Choices:

- Prioritize the body.
- Inspect the record timestamp.
- Seek a second witness.
- Preserve all channels without resolving the contradiction.
- Flag the official record as unreliable.
- Continue rescue before analysis.

The system should not reduce this to a simplistic right-or-wrong quiz.

Different choices should unlock:

- different evidence;
- different doctrine principles;
- different annotations;
- different unresolved questions;
- different final witness-ledger language.

The system may identify consequences, but it should not shame the audience for choosing caution, verification, or preservation of uncertainty.

---

## 4. Consequences of Choice

Audience choices should affect what the reader knows and how the final record is assembled.

Choices may affect:

- which fragments unlock;
- which character dossier deepens;
- which Rescue Doctrine steps become visible;
- which contradictions are identified;
- which evidence is marked trusted, disputed, or unresolved;
- which testimony is centered;
- what the final witness ledger contains;
- which archive routes are recommended on replay.

Choices should not affect locked events.

---

## 5. What the Audience Must Not Be Allowed to Change

The audience must not be able to:

- prevent the Cape Wipeout;
- make Ada die;
- decide that Ada was never injured;
- save or kill KC;
- replace Tallai’s rescue decision;
- prevent Q from detecting life;
- prevent Quartz from carrying Ada;
- reveal the Reporter’s face;
- rewrite the Council’s history;
- change the identities of the survivors;
- convert unresolved canon into a false answer;
- create alternate-history endings.

The audience investigates history.

The audience does not manufacture a different history.

---

## 6. Recommended Episode Architecture

## Fixed Anchor 1 — Council Era

This section remains controlled.

Required beats:

1. The factions gather.
2. Leadership of Umada and Tisetan is disputed.
3. Ada refuses the time-travel assignment.
4. Ada chooses the Reporter.
5. The Omoluabi device is transferred.
6. The Reporter activates it.
7. The record opens onto Friedmandorstrop.

The audience may inspect evidence around the Council, but the event order should remain fixed.

---

## Interactive Field 1 — Friedmandorstrop Before

The audience chooses a starting route:

- Tallai;
- garden;
- Ada;
- Reporter/device.

The system tracks:

- locations visited;
- witnesses encountered;
- environmental anomalies observed;
- evidence channels opened;
- whether the audience has seen Ada’s KIA record before convergence.

The audience should be free to visit more than one route before the countdown converges.

Recommended behavior:

- one route is selected first;
- after completing it, the user may choose another route;
- the system may warn that time is narrowing;
- Tick Four eventually forces convergence.

---

## Fixed Anchor 2 — Tick Four

Ada sees the premature KIA record.

This is a required historical event.

However, the meaning of the moment changes depending on what the audience has already witnessed.

For example:

- a garden-first reader may already distrust official sensors;
- a Tallai-first reader may understand care as a civic practice;
- an Ada-first reader may experience the event as personal betrayal;
- a Reporter-first reader may recognize temporal corruption.

---

## Fixed Anchor 3 — Tick Five and BOOM

Tick Five and BOOM remain fixed.

The audience cannot avoid the blast.

The interface may change based on prior routes:

- different fragments may rupture;
- different evidence may remain accessible;
- different channels may fail;
- different witness signals may appear first.

---

## Interactive Field 2 — Rescue

The audience chooses which signal to follow:

- Q;
- Quartz;
- Tallai;
- Ada;
- official record;
- Reporter.

The audience may follow multiple signals.

Each signal reveals a different part of the rescue.

The full rescue record should emerge through accumulation rather than one omniscient paragraph.

---

## Fixed Anchor 4 — Ada Is Carried Out

The rescue outcome remains locked:

- Q detects life.
- Tallai orders extraction.
- Quartz carries Ada.
- Ada remains listed KIA.
- Ada asks for KC.
- The dusty teddy bear is preserved as part of the rescue image.

---

## Final Interactive Layer — Witness Ledger

At the end, the Omoluabi device generates a personalized witness ledger based on the audience’s path.

The ledger should summarize:

- routes taken;
- witnesses followed;
- evidence channels examined;
- contradictions identified;
- records trusted;
- records disputed;
- unresolved questions preserved;
- Rescue Doctrine steps revealed;
- evidence not yet examined.

Example structure:

> **WITNESS LEDGER — CAPE WIPEOUT, PT. 1**
>
> Routes examined: Garden, Ada  
> Witnesses followed: Q, Tallai  
> Record contradictions found: 3  
> Official status disputed: KIA  
> Bodily evidence prioritized: Yes  
> Unresolved question preserved: Location of KC  
> Doctrine revealed: Analysis, Extraction, Witness  
> Unexamined fragment: Quartz rescue path

The final ledger should invite replay without implying failure.

Example:

> This record remains incomplete. Another route may reveal evidence you did not witness.

## 7. Branching Without Narrative Chaos

Use a hub-and-convergence structure.

### Recommended pattern

1. Fixed anchor.
2. Audience chooses among branches.
3. Each branch reveals unique evidence.
4. Branches return to a central decision point.
5. A fixed historical event forces convergence.
6. New branches open after the event.
7. Final canon outcome remains unchanged.
8. The witness ledger reflects the chosen path.

This is preferable to:

- fully linear progression;
- unlimited random fragment order on first reading;
- traditional choose-your-own-adventure endings;
- binary moral quizzes;
- cosmetic buttons that reveal equivalent content.

---

## 8. Choice Design Principles

Every meaningful choice should answer at least one of these questions:

- What does the audience learn?
- Whose perspective is centered?
- Which contradiction becomes visible?
- Which doctrine principle becomes legible?
- What remains unresolved?
- How does the final witness ledger change?

Do not include a choice when every option leads immediately to identical content without interpretive consequence.

---

## 9. Interface Recommendations

### Route-selection cards

Use clear cards with:

- route title;
- witness or location;
- type of evidence;
- estimated fragment count;
- status: unseen, partial, complete;
- accessible button label.

Example:

> **ENTER THE GARDEN**  
> Environmental evidence · Q detection signal  
> Status: Unseen

---

### Evidence-channel controls

Maintain BODY / RECORD, but add:

- WITNESS;
- ENVIRONMENT;
- DEVICE.

Do not show every channel at every moment.

Only offer channels relevant to the fragment.

---

### Evidence map

Add an accessible evidence map or ledger panel showing:

- fragments collected;
- contradictions;
- witnesses;
- doctrine steps;
- unresolved questions.

The map should be available as both:

- a visual diagram or node map;
- a linear semantic list for screen-reader and keyboard users.

---

### Revisit controls

After a branch is completed, allow:

- return to route hub;
- continue toward convergence;
- review collected evidence;
- compare contradictions.

---

### Archive Mode

After the first completed witness ledger, unlock full Archive Mode.

Archive Mode should allow:

- free fragment selection;
- filtering by witness;
- filtering by location;
- filtering by evidence type;
- filtering by doctrine;
- comparison of BODY and RECORD;
- revisiting skipped branches;
- generating a revised witness ledger.

---

## 10. Accessibility Requirements for Interactive Choice

Every branch and choice must be fully accessible.

Required:

- keyboard-operable route cards;
- visible focus;
- proper heading structure;
- clear focus movement after a choice;
- announcements when a fragment, doctrine step, or contradiction unlocks;
- no drag-only interaction;
- no hover-only information;
- no sound-only evidence;
- textual equivalents for environmental and device signals;
- screen-reader-readable evidence map;
- no color-only state indicators;
- persistent text size and contrast preferences;
- saved progress;
- a restart option;
- a review-my-path option.

When a user chooses a route, announce:

> Garden route selected. Environmental fragment one of three opened.

When a contradiction is discovered, announce:

> Contradiction added to witness ledger: official record says KIA; body signal remains responsive.

---

## 11. Suggested State Model

Track at minimum:

```js
state = {
  routesVisited: [],
  fragmentsSeen: [],
  witnessesFollowed: [],
  evidenceChannelsOpened: [],
  contradictionsFound: [],
  doctrineUnlocked: [],
  unresolvedQuestions: [],
  trustDecisions: [],
  currentAnchor: "",
  firstReadingComplete: false
}
```

Persist the state locally so the episode survives reloads.

Also provide:

- Restart episode.
- Clear witness ledger.
- Continue previous record.
- Begin a new investigation.

## 13. Updated Direction to Claude

Do not simplify Episode 1 into a linear first reading with decorative interactions.

Revise the current build so that:

- the Council opening remains a fixed anchor;
- Friedmandorstrop becomes a branching investigation field;
- the audience chooses route, witness, and evidence;
- Tick Four, Tick Five, and BOOM remain fixed historical anchors;
- the rescue becomes a multi-perspective investigation;
- choices change knowledge, doctrine, dossier depth, and the final ledger;
- choices do not change locked canon;
- Archive Mode becomes fully open after completion;
- the ending generates a personalized witness ledger;
- every branch is keyboard and screen-reader accessible.

The governing interaction principle is:

> **The audience may choose what to examine, whom to believe provisionally, and which contradictions to preserve. The audience may not rewrite history.**

The governing dramatic question is:

> **When the record and the living body disagree, how do you decide what deserves to be believed?**
