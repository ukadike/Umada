# UMADA — Episode 1 Interactive Reader Review and Revision Brief

**Artifact under review:** `episode-interactive-wip (1).html`  
**Purpose:** Provide Claude Code with a clear revision brief for the Episode 1 interactive reader.  
**Status:** WIP review; not yet approved as the definitive canonical release.

---

## 1. Executive Assessment

The current prototype is a strong **UMADA archival interface**, but it is not yet a complete Episode 1 experience.

Its strongest elements are:

- the **BODY / RECORD** interaction;
- the visual language of a recovered historical archive;
- the ADA-1 ethical principles;
- the use of incomplete fragments rather than invented canon;
- the protection of the Reporter / Time Traveler’s identity.

Its main weakness is structural:

> The production notes, dossiers, and canon commentary are more developed than the actual dramatic episode.

The interface currently behaves more like a canon database than a finished Episode 1 reader. The revision should preserve the archival intelligence while making the episode emotionally legible, paced, cinematic, and accessible.

## 2. Preserve These Core Strengths

### 2.1 BODY / RECORD is the central interaction

This is the strongest mechanic in the prototype.

Ada’s official military record identifies her as dead while her body remains alive. The user can switch between the institutional record and bodily evidence.

This directly expresses:

- Trust Body Before Record.
- Trust Witness Before Narrative.
- Trust Survival Before Authority.

Do not remove or trivialize this mechanic. Expand it carefully so that it becomes the governing interaction of Episode 1.

### 2.2 Preserve the archival visual language

Keep the current foundation:

- warm paper background;
- dark ink typography;
- restrained signal color;
- serif narrative text;
- monospaced archival labels;
- fragment language;
- documentary and evidentiary framing.

The page should continue to feel like:

- a recovered historical record;
- a future civic archive;
- an Omoluabi witness device;
- a civilization reconstructing incomplete memory.

It should not become a conventional entertainment landing page.

### 2.3 Preserve the Reporter’s obscured identity

The Reporter / Time Traveler’s face must remain unknown.

Approved visual constraints include:

- rear silhouette;
- hood;
- shadow;
- gloved hands;
- partial framing;
- no frontal face reveal.

### 2.4 Preserve canon restraint

Continue marking genuinely missing material as:

> AWAITING FRAGMENT

Do not invent plot, biography, motives, or visual canon to fill gaps. However, do not use “awaiting fragment” as a substitute for writing scenes whose canon is already established.

# 3. Primary Structural Change

## Separate the episode from the production ledger

The current prototype mixes audience-facing narrative with internal source control, filename references, canon-index dates, spelling disputes, and repository notes.

Create two clearly distinct modes.

### Story Mode

The cinematic Episode 1 experience.

Story Mode should include:

- controlled progression;
- concise dramatic prose;
- visual and audio cues;
- interactive BODY / RECORD moments;
- character actions;
- scene transitions;
- limited exposition;
- no repository filenames or open-question numbers in the main reading flow.

### Canon Ledger

A secondary production and provenance layer.

Canon Ledger may include:

- locked canon;
- emerging canon;
- open questions;
- source package names;
- provenance;
- version dates;
- alternate spellings;
- deprecated terms;
- internal production notes.

The existing “DOSSIERS” tab can become part of the Canon Ledger, but its public-facing presentation should be simplified.

# 4. Canon Corrections Required

These are required before release.

## 4.1 Correct the place name globally

The locked spelling is:

> **Friedmandorstrop**

The prototype currently uses `Friedmandostorp` in multiple locations.

Correct it globally in:

- episode fragments;
- dossier text;
- source labels;
- UI copy;
- metadata;
- future scene descriptions;
- any generated asset references.

## 4.2 Lock Tallai’s spelling

The correct spelling is:

> **Tallai**

Do not present `Tallai / Talla` as an unresolved audience-facing question.

`Talla` may remain only in a migration note inside the Canon Ledger as a deprecated earlier variant.

## 4.3 Standardize KC naming

Use:

> **KC**

throughout Episode 1 unless a deliberate full-name reveal is later approved.

Do not mix:

- KC;
- Kasey;
- Casey;
- Kassey.

Spelling history may be documented in the Canon Ledger, but it should not interrupt the episode.

## 4.4 Revise the rescue phrasing

Current phrasing:

> Q and Quartz debate what to do with the body.

This is wrong in tone and implies Ada may be treated as a corpse.

Replace with something closer to:

> Q registers conflicting signs: the record identifies Ada as dead, but her body remains responsive. Quartz waits for Tallai’s direction. Tallai orders extraction.

The disagreement must be about evidence and procedure, not whether Ada deserves care.

## 4.5 Clarify the rescue destination

Current phrasing:

> They take Ada to the lab.

This is ambiguous because the laboratory has just been attacked.

The story must define whether they take her to:

- an intact emergency wing;
- an underground care station;
- a protected garden facility;
- Tallai’s refuge;
- another established safe site.

Do not finalize this detail by guessing. Mark it as a specific canon decision required.

## 4.6 Review “Care Shooter”

The Ada dossier currently uses the phrase:

> Care Shooter

This does not clearly align with Ada’s established role as a field commander and may be an animatic credit rather than an in-world designation.

Remove it from the public dossier unless an approved definition exists.

The existing line:

> The houses meet.

is insufficient.

Episode 1 must establish the Council Era with a real scene.

Include the following established elements:

- representatives or leaders of the factions;
- the circular council chamber;
- Umada and Tisetan as the subject of the leadership dispute;
- speech, Nago, prosthetic gesture, and tactile communication functioning as normal civic language;
- Ada refusing the time-travel assignment;
- the Reporter being selected instead;

- activation of the device;

The device transfer should feel ceremonial, political, and consequential.

# 6. Clarify the Reader’s Role

The existing introduction says:

> You carry an Omoluabi device.

Canon says the Reporter carries the device.

Choose one of these models and apply it consistently.

## Preferred model: the reader accesses the Reporter’s record

Recommended copy:

> You are entering an Omoluabi record carried by the Reporter. You may examine what was witnessed. You may not author what occurred.

This preserves the Reporter as a distinct character while allowing the user to investigate the record.

## Alternative model: the reader is temporarily embodied as the Reporter

This can work only if the entire interface consistently treats the user as the Reporter.

Recommended copy:

> You have been selected to carry an Omoluabi device. You may witness the record. You may not rewrite it.

Do not mix these two identities.

# 7. Rebuild the Five Ticks as Five Separate Scenes

The current prototype compresses all five ticks into one fragment.

That removes the intended escalation.

Create five distinct beats.

## Tick One — Garden

- establish the living laboratory garden;
- introduce a minor disturbance;
- leaves or sensors react;
- Q detects something before anyone else.

## Tick Two — Bread Route

- Tallai moves through Friedmandorstrop;
- Q and Quartz accompany Tallai;
- ordinary civilian life continues;
- the bread route feels familiar and routine.

## Tick Three — Ada

- Ada arrives early;
- she expects to meet KC;
- she checks the time;
- she is still operating inside ordinary procedure.

## Tick Four — Record

Ada sees:

> FIELD COMMANDER ADA  
> STATUS: KIA

The record predates final blast confirmation.

This should be a major interactive BODY / RECORD moment.

## Tick Five — Recognition

- Ada looks up;
- Q reacts;
- the Reporter’s device registers an anomaly;
- the environment shifts from unease to imminent catastrophe.

## BOOM

The blast interrupts the sequence.

The page, sound design, typography, and record state may rupture together.

# 8. Remove the Unintentional Duplicate BOOM

The prototype currently presents:

1. five ticks and BOOM in the entry overlay;
2. another “Five ticks” fragment;
3. another BOOM fragment.

This weakens the event.

Recommended solution:

- use one distant or isolated tick on entry;
- place the full five-tick sequence inside the episode;
- reserve BOOM for the narrative climax.

An alternative memory-loop structure is possible, but the duplication must then be deliberately explained.

This preserves both cinematic pacing and investigative rereading.

# 11. Rewrite the Dossiers for Audience Use

The current dossiers are useful as production records but too dense for the public reader.

They currently include:

- source filenames;
- dates;
- package references;
- open-question numbers;
- spelling disputes;
- long provenance explanations.

Move those details into a collapsible:

> PROVENANCE / EDITOR NOTES

Public dossier copy should be short and legible.

## Example: Ada

> **ADA — FIELD COMMANDER**  
> One of the known survivors of the Cape Wipeout. Officially recorded as killed before the blast occurred. Later underwent the first complete cybernetic reconstruction. In the Council Era, she refuses the role of time traveler and selects the Reporter.

## Example: Tallai

> **TALLAI — CHIMERA CARE SPECIALIST**  
> Known along the Friedmandorstrop bread route before the Wipeout. Tallai discovers Ada alive after the blast and orders her extraction despite the official record. Later becomes steward of Ada’s Estate of Memory.

## Example: Q

> **Q — DETECTION AND SENSING**  
> A small insect-like chimera whose sensory response identifies danger and bodily survival before the official systems do.

## Example: Quartz

> **QUARTZ — CARRIER AND RESCUE ANCHOR**  
> A massive, gentle protector who clears debris and carries Ada from the blast site.

## Remove empty Episode 1 dossiers

Do not display Aisha, Nayla, or Adeola in the Episode 1 public dossier view until they have an actual role in this episode.

They may remain in the Canon Ledger.

# 12. Activate the Rescue Doctrine

The six-step Rescue Doctrine is a strong framework:

1. Extraction
2. Stabilization
3. Care
4. Analysis
5. Transport
6. Witness

However, all six steps currently say `AWAITING FRAGMENT`.

The doctrine should be revealed through the rescue itself.

## Recommended behavior

- keep the Doctrine tab locked or partially obscured at first;
- unlock each step after the corresponding story action;
- show how the doctrine emerged from actual practice;
- preserve missing procedural detail as awaiting fragment where necessary.

## Suggested baseline definitions

### 1. Extraction

Remove the living being from immediate danger without allowing the official record to determine whether rescue is warranted.

### 2. Stabilization

Protect breath, circulation, structural integrity, sensory continuity, and immediate bodily survival.

### 3. Care

Treat personhood as present even when speech, movement, identity records, or institutional status are unavailable.

### 4. Analysis

Examine conflicting evidence without delaying life-preserving action.

### 5. Transport

Move the survivor using the least harmful available route and method.

### 6. Witness

Preserve what the body, rescuers, environment, and record each reveal, including contradictions between them.

These definitions should be treated as draft language unless already confirmed elsewhere in canon.

# 13. Accessibility Requirements

The current prototype has a promising foundation but does not yet meet UMADA’s accessibility standard.

## 13.1 Fix keyboard operation of the intro

The intro uses:

- `role="button"`
- `tabindex="0"`

but responds only to click.

Add activation for:

- Enter;
- Space.

Also provide a real visible button such as:

> ENTER EPISODE

Do not rely on the entire overlay as an implied button.

## 13.2 Announce dynamically revealed fragments

When a user pulls a fragment:

- move focus to the newly opened fragment heading; or
- announce the change through an appropriate live region.

A screen-reader user must know that new content appeared.

## 13.3 Add state semantics to BODY / RECORD controls

The current `.on` class is visual only.

Use:

- `aria-pressed`; or
- proper tab / tabpanel semantics.

The selected state must be available to assistive technology.

## 13.4 Add state semantics to main navigation

EPISODE, DOSSIERS, and DOCTRINE need a meaningful programmatic state.

Use either:

- a proper tabs pattern with `role="tablist"`, `role="tab"`, `aria-selected`, and linked tab panels; or
- ordinary section buttons followed by focus movement to the new section heading.

Do not simulate tabs incompletely.

## 13.5 Increase small text sizes

The current interface uses many 10–11px labels.

Increase normal interface text to a more readable minimum.

Recommended baseline:

- metadata and labels: at least 12–14px;
- body text: at least 16–18px;
- comfortable line height;
- responsive scaling.

## 13.6 Correct contrast

Current low-contrast values include approximately:

- `#8b877e` on `#e9e6df`: about 2.87:1;
- `#b3542e` on `#e9e6df`: about 3.99:1.

Normal text should meet at least 4.5:1 contrast.

Adjust:

- muted text;
- signal text;
- borders where they convey state;
- focus indicators;
- inactive labels.

Do not use color alone to communicate BODY / RECORD state.

## 13.7 Add intro controls

The timed intro should offer:

- Skip;
- Pause;
- Replay;
- a text equivalent;
- reduced-motion behavior;
- no forced audio;
- no loss of content when motion is disabled.

## 13.8 Preserve reading position and focus

When switching tabs or rendering content:

- do not unexpectedly return focus to the top;
- preserve logical reading order;
- expose a clear section heading;
- support browser zoom;
- avoid fixed-height content regions.

# 14. Recommended Definitive Episode 1 Structure

## Prologue — The Council Era

- faction representatives meet;
- Umada and Tisetan leadership is disputed;
- communication is multimodal and civic;
- Ada refuses the assignment;
- the Reporter is chosen;
- the Luabi / Omoluabi device is transferred;
- the Reporter activates it.

## Act I — Friedmandorstrop Before

- establish date and place;
- show the garden, laboratory, bread route, and town;
- Tallai, Q, and Quartz move through their morning;
- Ada arrives early;
- Ada expects KC.

## Act II — The Five Ticks

- each tick is a separate scene;
- ordinary life narrows toward danger;
- Tick Four reveals Ada’s premature KIA record;
- Tick Five confirms an anomaly.

## Act III — Cape Wipeout

- BOOM;
- the Reporter is destabilized;
- Ada survives;
- the record remains KIA;
- the town and garden are ruptured.

## Act IV — Rescue

- Q detects life;
- Quartz clears debris;
- Tallai orders extraction;
- the Rescue Doctrine is enacted;
- Quartz encounters the dusty teddy bear;
- Ada asks for KC;
- the official record remains unchanged.

## Closing fragment

Return briefly to the Reporter, the record, or the Council Era.

Suggested closing language:

> The record says she died.  
> The civilization that followed began when someone refused to believe it.

Treat this line as proposed language, not locked canon, until approved.

- Do not remove the BODY / RECORD mechanic.
- Do not reveal the Reporter’s face.
- Do not invent missing canon.
- Do not present internal repository disputes as the main audience experience.

- Do not use “awaiting fragment” where established story actions can already be written.
