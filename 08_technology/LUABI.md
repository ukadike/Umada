# Luabi

**Canon status:** LOCKED — lineage and interface doctrine  
**Date locked:** 2026-09-01  
**Scope of lock:** name/lineage distinction, design philosophy, human-interface relationship  
**Capability details:** remain governed by existing UMADA canon and no-fabrication rules

## Canon distinction

**Luabi** is the fictional UMADA device / future descendant informed by the real-world **Omoluabi** research system.

They are not the same artifact.

- **Omoluabi** — present-day real-world Small Systems Lab research system and prototype environment for observation, evidence, accessibility, provenance, sensing, and human-governed interpretation.
- **Luabi** — fictional future UMADA device that can extrapolate from that research lineage within the bounds of UMADA canon.

This distinction supersedes informal usage that treated “Omoluabi” as the final in-world device name. Existing references should be migrated through normal canon-governance procedures rather than silently rewritten.

## Locked design principle

Luabi inherits the following design proposition:

> **The more advanced the system, the more intuitive the interface becomes.**

Within UMADA, technological sophistication should not automatically produce more menus, more screens, more visible controls, or more cognitive burden.

The more complex Luabi becomes internally, the more capable it should become of translating that complexity into an interaction appropriate to the person and situation.

## Complexity underneath, clarity at the surface

Luabi may coordinate complex sensing, records, environmental information, historical context, uncertainty, or other capabilities established elsewhere in canon. Those internal systems do not require the user to encounter all internal complexity at once.

A canonical interaction relationship is:

**NEED → UNDERSTANDING → ACTION → WHY → EVIDENCE / DEEPER INQUIRY**

The exact future interface remains a design space. This document locks the relationship, not an unsupported list of fictional features.

## Not an oracle

Intuitiveness does not mean unquestionable authority.

Luabi's sophistication should preserve a person's ability to interrogate what the device is presenting. Where the device does not have sufficient basis for a determination, uncertainty or the need for another observation should remain expressible.

The fictional system should not become “advanced” merely by becoming more certain, more controlling, or more opaque.

## Human relationship

Luabi is most advanced when the human does not need to learn unnecessary machine complexity to use it.

That does not mean the human gives up agency. The design relationship is:

**human intention ↔ Luabi interpretation ↔ environment**

Human agency, questioning, and override remain central unless future canon explicitly establishes a conflict with those principles.

## Accessibility as technological sophistication

Accessibility is part of the device's conception of intelligence, not an add-on mode.

Luabi should be capable, where canon and scene requirements support it, of communicating through multiple forms rather than assuming one idealized body or sensory condition. Specific capabilities must still be established through normal canon workflow and must not be invented solely because this principle exists.

## Machinery of usefulness

Luabi also inherits a material-design rule:

> **Form follows usefulness.**

Future machinery in UMADA should reveal the human, environmental, survival, repair, access, or civic problem that caused it to be built. “Futuristic” appearance by itself is not a design rationale.

A companion hardware rule is:

> **Capability can expand without interaction complexity expanding with it.**

## Failure history

UMADA's histories of infrastructural failure, including Dead Glass, make resilience, physical legibility, repair, redundancy, and non-screen-dependent interaction meaningful design concerns. This principle does not establish new Dead Glass capabilities; it governs how future technology development should be reasoned about when those conditions are relevant.

## Real-world research bridge — production note, not in-world canon

The real-world Omoluabi project can prototype interface, sensing, accessibility, provenance, and human-control concepts that later inform Luabi development.

The relationship is:

**OMOLUABI — real-world research → design learning → speculative translation → LUABI — UMADA future artifact**

Nothing from the real-world repository automatically becomes UMADA canon. It must still pass through UMADA's governance and fragment workflow.

## Cultural/design provenance — production research, not in-world canon

Two cultural observations helped articulate this design direction:

- *Green Lantern: Beware My Power* (2022): at approximately the 16-minute mark, Green Arrow refers to an idea attributed to Hal Jordan about advanced interfaces becoming more intuitive. Exact dialogue remains to be verified before direct quotation.
- *Aliens* (1986): industrial and rescue machinery provides a useful visual precedent for machinery whose form communicates function.

These works are **design stimuli**, not evidence inside the UMADA world and not sources of UMADA canon facts.

## Research boundary

The Small Systems Lab method for these references is:

**FICTION / CULTURAL OBSERVATION → QUESTION → DESIGN PRINCIPLE → REAL-WORLD RESEARCH → PROTOTYPE / TESTING → EVIDENCE → GOVERNED SPECULATIVE TRANSLATION**

This pathway allows speculative fiction to function as a research environment without confusing fictional precedent with scientific validation.

## Canon lock summary

Locked as of 2026-09-01:

1. The fictional device is **Luabi**; Omoluabi is the real-world research lineage.
2. Luabi's advancement is expressed partly through increasing intuitiveness rather than increasing visible interface complexity.
3. Simplicity does not require opacity or unquestionable authority.
4. Accessibility is a marker of technological sophistication, not an afterthought.
5. Form follows usefulness.
6. Capability may expand without interaction complexity expanding.
7. Real-world Omoluabi research may inform Luabi, but cross-project material does not automatically become canon.
